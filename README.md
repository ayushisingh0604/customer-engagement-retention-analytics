## Project Overview
This project focuses on analysing customer engagement, product utilisation, customer relationship indicators, and churn patterns within the banking sector. The objective is to identify customer segments associated with higher attrition and develop data-driven recommendations to support customer retention strategies.

This is a **Financial Analytics Live Project** completed under **Unified Mentor**, using Microsoft Excel and Power BI.

## Project Objectives
- Analyse customer churn and retention patterns.
- Understand customer engagement and activity levels.
- Examine churn across age groups, gender, and geography.
- Analyse product utilisation and customer relationship tiers.
- Identify customer segments with higher attrition rates.
- Develop actionable recommendations for improving retention.

## Key Findings

### Geographic Analysis
Germany showed a churn rate of **32.44%**, significantly higher than France (**16.15%**) and Spain (**16.67%**).

### Age Analysis
Customers aged 45–54 and 55–64 demonstrated notably higher churn rates than younger customer groups.

### Customer Engagement
Inactive customers had a churn rate of **26.85%**, compared with **14.27%** among active customers.

### Product Utilisation
Customers with three products had a churn rate of **82.71%**, while the four-product group had **100% churn**. However, these groups were small, so findings should be interpreted cautiously.

### Relationship Analysis
Customers classified in the custom Weak Relationship Tier had higher churn (**28.17%**) compared with Moderate (**19.87%**) and Strong (**11.80%**) tiers.

## Business Recommendations
- Investigate customer experience and retention challenges in Germany.
- Develop engagement strategies for inactive customers.
- Prioritise retention efforts for higher-churn age segments.
- Investigate product suitability and customer satisfaction among multi-product customers.
- Strengthen customer relationship engagement.
- Monitor churn, retention, and engagement KPIs regularly.
- Use additional financial data to measure the impact of retention initiatives.

## Limitations
The dataset does not contain actual revenue, profit, Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), acquisition costs, or retention campaign costs. Therefore, the project identifies churn patterns and business opportunities but does not calculate actual financial impact.

RelationshipScore and RelationshipTier are custom analytical indicators created for this project. The findings represent observed associations and do not establish causation.

## Conclusion
This project demonstrates the use of Excel and Power BI to analyse customer engagement, product utilisation, and churn patterns in banking. The analysis supports identifying higher-risk customer segments and developing targeted, data-driven retention recommendations.
